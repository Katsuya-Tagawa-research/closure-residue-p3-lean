## 2. Theorem R-G2 (finite compositional access semantics, CONDITIONAL EXACT)

For each admissible labelled word \(w=(a_1,\dots,a_n)\), including instrument outcomes along a legal schedule, define its CP semantics by ordered composition:
\[
\mathcal E_w=\mathcal E_{a_n}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal E_{a_1}.
\]
For prepared density \(\rho\) and allowed terminal effect \(E_r\),
\[
p_{\mathsf B}(r,w\mid\rho)
=\operatorname{Tr}[E_r\,\mathcal E_w(\rho)].
\]
Every such branch probability is nonnegative. The appropriate *complete* tree of instrument/readout outcomes at a prescribed causal experiment has total probability one, provided instrument alternatives are complete and no branches are discarded. This operational semantics is **unique** after the generator meanings, schedule and readouts are fixed, by induction on the length of legal histories. Replacing arbitrary postselected subfamilies by "normalized" experiments is explicitly forbidden. The resulting record map is linear on the span of allowed input preparations and can be fed into H82's predictive quotient theorem under its independent finite/linear hypotheses.

**Proof:** complete positivity is preserved by composition; the sum of all branches of a complete instrument is TP, and summing over complete terminal POVM effects returns the trace. Induction over the legal rooted decision tree gives the total. Uniqueness follows from the recursive definition \(\mathcal E_{wa}=\mathcal E_a\circ\mathcal E_w\), with \(\mathcal E_\emptyset=\mathrm{id}\). The declared schedule limits the domain. This is a standard quantum-instrument composition result, not F's generative law.

**Failure of intrinsic inference:** if \(\mathsf B\) is independently supplied, the existence of the finite evaluator says nothing about whether \(\mathsf B\) is physically available, or uniquely generated by the channel, P3 or F. Labelled unitary logarithms and exact CPTP Stinespring constructions remain only mathematical resources unless their hardware provenance is separately established.

## 3. Theorem R-G3 (source-fibre selector criterion)

Let \(\mathfrak D\) be a declared family of devices, \(s:\mathfrak D\to\mathfrak S\) a projection to source-only data, and \(a:\mathfrak D\to\mathfrak O\) the desired observational equivalence class. For an **independently specified** proposed boundary-data map \(b:\mathfrak D\to\mathfrak B\), a deterministic selector \(\sigma:\operatorname{im}(s,b)\to\mathfrak O\) with
\[
a=\sigma\circ(s,b)
\]
exists if and only if
\[
s(d)=s(d'),\ b(d)=b(d')\ \Longrightarrow\ a(d)=a(d')
\]
for all \(d,d'\in\mathfrak D\).

**Proof:** necessity is direct; sufficiency defines \(\sigma(s(d),b(d))=a(d)\), which is well defined exactly under fibre constancy. Uniqueness holds **on the image only**, not on unobserved source/boundary combinations. Neither the mere existence of \(\sigma\) nor a choice of \(b\) establishes that the boundary data were generated by F.

**Minimal label corollary, source-fibre scoped:** if a fixed source fibre contains \(k\) different operational classes, then every sufficient selector boundary-data variable \(b\) must take at least \(k\) different values on that fibre. Any fixed-width binary encoding of its values needs at least \(\lceil\log_2 k\rceil\) bits. This is *only* a counting lower bound for a selected family, not an entropy/thermodynamic cost, quantum communication theorem, or universal minimum axiom count.
